Hospital & Clinic System

One live Django system for reception, doctors, laboratory, imaging, pharmacy and billing at a private hospital — from patient token to report.

Problem

Busy OPD hours meant crowded waiting areas, handwritten tokens, lost lab slips and patients walking between departments to ask whether a report was ready. Doctors could not see a patient’s tests and earlier prescriptions in one place.

Users

Receptionists, doctors, nurses, laboratory staff, billing staff and managers — each with a separate role — plus patients through their own portal.

My role

I designed, built and maintain the system end to end: the clinical and financial data model, department workflows, printed documents, permissions and tests, working directly with hospital staff on how each department actually operates.

Implemented features

  • Reception: patient registration, appointments and doctor schedules, daily tokens per department with printed token slips.
  • OPD: visits, medical records, consultation templates, and prescriptions printed on the hospital letterhead.
  • Laboratory and imaging: test orders, results with parameters, and tracking of X-ray, MRI and ultrasound procedures.
  • Indoor care: admissions and rooms, nursing medication, surgeries and implants, physiotherapy, dressing and injection services.
  • Pharmacy and stock: a pharmacy point of sale with medicine stock, and internal inventory for consumables.
  • Money: invoices, payments, refunds, cash reconciliation and an immutable day-end audit snapshot.
  • Staff: attendance, exceptions and payroll.
  • Clinical safety: drug-interaction, allergy and contraindication warnings while prescribing, with every override recorded in an audit log.

Architecture decisions

  • One shared visit record. Reception, doctor, lab, imaging, pharmacy and billing all work on the same visit, so nothing is retyped and nothing travels on paper.
  • Separate Django apps by responsibility: the core hospital workflow, clinical safety, pharmacy point of sale and internal inventory.
  • Clinical-safety checks as small APIs (Django REST Framework): drug interactions, allergy checks and contraindications are checked while the doctor prescribes, and overrides are posted back with a reason.
  • SQLite today, PostgreSQL ready. Setting one environment variable moves the system to PostgreSQL with no code change.

Security and data handling

  • Role-based access with checks on the server, covered by automated tests — for example: a patient cannot list other patients, only a manager can see payroll, and only billing staff can turn an order into an invoice — a nurse or a patient trying is refused.
  • Audit trails that are never edited: the activity log, day-end audit snapshots, and the clinical override log.
  • The secret key comes from an environment variable or a private file on the server; debug mode is off.
  • Public material uses sample patients only; no live address or login is published.

Challenges and lessons

  • Departments see the same patient differently. Making the visit the shared record, with lab, imaging, pharmacy and billing orders attached to it, means no department retypes another’s data.
  • Money needs an audit trail, not just totals. Day-end snapshots cannot be edited after they are taken, so the day’s figures can be checked later against what was recorded at the time.
  • Clinical warnings must not block care silently. A doctor can override a warning, but must give a reason, and the override is logged.
  • Reproducible setup matters. Pinning the project’s dependencies let the full test suite run cleanly from a fresh environment.

Evidence

68 automated tests pass, including an access-control suite. The demo video uses sample patients. Source code is private because the system holds patient records; a walkthrough on a demo database is available on request.
